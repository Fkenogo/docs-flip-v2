## 1. Purpose

This document captures the current strategic concept for Docsflip before detailed product, commercial, operational, and engineering design begins.

It records what Docsflip is intended to become, who it is for, the problem it addresses, its core value proposition, its proposed commercial direction, and the principles that should guide future development.

This document is not a technical specification, pricing schedule, implementation plan, or final business model. It provides the product concept baseline from which those documents can be developed.

---

## 2. Concept Summary

Docsflip is an Africa-focused digital publishing platform that enables users to turn PDF documents into interactive, shareable digital publications.

The platform is designed around a pay-as-you-publish model rather than a conventional tiered software subscription model.

Users purchase Docsflip Credits and decide when and how to use them. Credits are consumed only when the user creates, changes, enhances, distributes, hosts, or exports a publication output.

Routine account and document-management actions remain free.

Docsflip will position itself as:

> **Africa’s pay-as-you-publish platform.**

Its primary customer proposition is:

> **Turn any PDF into a shareable digital publication from as little as $1. Publish when you need to. Pay only for what you use.**

East Africa will serve as the initial launch pad, but the product, brand, payment architecture, and long-term market ambition will be Africa-wide.

## 3. Origin of the Idea

Docsflip emerged from a practical recurring need observed while serving communications clients.

Organisations regularly produce PDF documents such as:

- annual reports;
- company profiles;
- brochures;
- magazines;
- newsletters;
- policy documents;
- programme reports;
- research publications;
- catalogues;
- event programmes;
- newspapers; and
- public information materials.

These organisations often want to present and distribute the documents in a more engaging digital format.

Existing international flipbook platforms demonstrate the value of this service, but their commercial models, subscription structures, payment options, and product assumptions are not always well suited to African users and organisations.

Docsflip is therefore not intended to be a direct copy of an existing international platform. Its opportunity lies in combining digital publishing capability with a commercial and payment model designed around the realities of African markets.

---

## 4. The Problem

Many organisations already have valuable content in PDF format but lack a simple, affordable, and flexible way to turn those documents into interactive digital publications.

Common limitations include:

- subscription plans that force users to pay every month even when they publish occasionally;
- feature tiers that make users pay for functions they may not need;
- limited support for African payment methods;
- unclear pricing and hidden usage restrictions;
- products designed for international payment and operating environments;
- poor transparency on what the customer is paying for;
- weak support for organisations that publish periodically rather than continuously;
- limited flexibility between platform hosting, website embedding, and self-hosted outputs; and
- products that treat African markets as secondary rather than central.

This creates a gap for a platform that allows users to publish when required, pay only for the output they choose, and clearly understand how their money or credits are being used.

---

## 5. The Opportunity

Across Africa, organisations continue to produce large volumes of PDF-based content.

This includes:

- publishing houses digitising magazines and newspapers;
- NGOs publishing reports, evaluations, policy briefs, newsletters, and training materials;
- corporate organisations publishing annual reports, profiles, brochures, catalogues, and sustainability reports;
- government and public institutions publishing strategies, reports, policies, and public information documents;
- development organisations publishing research, programme documentation, and impact reports; and
- independent publishers and creators distributing magazines, portfolios, catalogues, and digital books.

Most of these users do not need a full-time digital publishing subscription.

They need a reliable way to convert a document, publish it, share it, host it for a defined period, and purchase additional outputs only when required.

Docsflip can serve this need through a transparent, output-based publishing model.

## 6. Product Vision

Docsflip will make digital publishing accessible to African organisations, publishers, and creators by giving them a simple way to transform existing PDF content into interactive publications without committing to an unnecessary recurring subscription.

The platform should allow a user to:

1. upload a PDF;
2. preview the publication;
3. see the credit cost;
4. select optional publication outputs;
5. approve the credit usage;
6. publish the document;
7. share, embed, host, or export it; and
8. monitor basic publication performance.

The product should feel controlled, transparent, and easy to understand.

The user should always know:

- what they are receiving;
- what is included;
- which actions are free;
- which actions consume credits;
- how many credits will be used;
- how many credits remain; and
- when hosting or other time-bound outputs will expire.

## 8. Core Product Principles

### 8.1 Pay for Outputs

Credits should be used only when an action creates, changes, enhances, distributes, hosts, or exports a publication output.

A user should not be charged merely for navigating or managing the platform.

### 8.2 Transparency

Before any credits are consumed, the user should see:

- the selected output;
- the credit cost;
- the resulting total;
- and the remaining balance after approval.

After each transaction, the user should receive confirmation of the credits used.

### 8.3 User Control

The user decides:

- when to publish;
- which outputs to add;
- how long to host the publication;
- whether to use Docsflip hosting;
- whether to embed it on another website;
- and whether to purchase a self-hosted package.

### 8.4 Simplicity

The platform should avoid unnecessary product tiers and licence levels.

A user should not need to understand Starter, Professional, Premium, or similar packages in order to publish a document.

### 8.5 Value Before Complexity

The first product should focus on the essential publishing flow.

Advanced features should be added only when they serve a clear user need or commercial purpose.

### 8.6 Africa-Wide by Design

The product should be designed for African expansion from the beginning, even where launch operations start in a limited number of countries.

This includes:

- configurable payment methods;
- country-aware payment support;
- support for mobile money;
- card and bank-payment options;
- invoice-based organisational payment;
- configurable taxes and currencies;
- and payment-provider abstraction.

### 8.7 Engineering Guided by the Founder Engineering Framework

Docsflip should benefit from the emerging Founder Engineering Framework.

The project should not independently recreate extensive governance or engineering structures that the FEF is intended to provide across Founder projects.

Docsflip should define its own product and commercial needs clearly, then use the FEF to guide engineering governance, implementation discipline, assurance, traceability, and controlled delivery.

## 9. The Docsflip Credit Model

Docsflip Credits are the internal value units users purchase and use to create publication outputs.

Purchased credits should not expire merely because time has passed.

Credits should be purchased in bundles.

The proposition “from as little as $1” communicates the value that a small amount of credit can create. It does not necessarily mean that every payment transaction must be exactly $1.

A possible commercial direction is:

- a low-cost introductory purchase, potentially from $2;
- a normal minimum initial bundle, potentially around $10;
- later top-ups in defined bundles such as $5 or $10;
- larger bundles for recurring publishers and institutions; and
- allocated credits within organisation subscriptions.

The exact credit values, bundle sizes, margins, payment fees, and publication costs remain to be defined in the Docsflip Credit Economy and Publication Output Model.

## 10. Free Actions and Credit-Consuming Actions

### 10.1 Actions That Should Normally Remain Free

Examples include:

- creating an account;
- uploading a draft PDF;
- renaming a draft;
- organising drafts into folders;
- editing descriptions;
- previewing estimated costs;
- copying an existing share link;
- reviewing publication analytics;
- viewing credit history;
- deleting drafts;
- managing account details; and
- reviewing hosting expiry information.

### 10.2 Actions That May Consume Credits

Examples include:

- publishing a new flipbook;
- publishing more than the included page allowance;
- replacing the source PDF;
- removing Docsflip branding;
- adding a custom logo;
- generating website embed output;
- generating an offline interactive flipbook package;
- generating a self-hosted publication package;
- renewing Docsflip hosting;
- activating advanced analytics or publication enhancements; and
- purchasing additional publication storage or distribution outputs.

The guiding question should always be:

> What publication output did the user receive?

If an action produces no meaningful output, it should not consume credits merely for the sake of monetisation.

---

## 11. Base Publication Concept

The initial working concept is that a base publication will support a PDF of up to 50 pages.

The base publication should include:

- an interactive flipbook;
- a public shareable link;
- a mobile-responsive viewer;
- standard Docsflip branding;
- basic publication controls;
- basic viewing analytics;
- and 12 months of Docsflip hosting.

Documents above 50 pages should use additional credits in blocks of 10 pages.

For example:

- 1–50 pages: base publication cost;
- 51–60 pages: one additional 10-page block;
- 61–70 pages: two additional blocks;
- and so forth.

The commercial viability of the 50-page base publication and the “from $1” proposition must be validated against conversion, storage, bandwidth, payment processing, support, and margin costs.

---

## 12. Hosting Model

The default publication should include 12 months of hosting on Docsflip.

The user should be able to see:

- the publication date;
- the hosting start date;
- the hosting expiry date;
- the renewal cost;
- the current publication status;
- and any renewal reminders.

After the included period, the user may renew hosting using a small number of credits.

The hosting lifecycle may include:

1. **Active** — the publication is available publicly.
2. **Renewal Due** — the user receives reminders.
3. **Suspended** — the public publication is unavailable but retained.
4. **Archived** — the publication is held for a recovery period.
5. **Deleted** — removal occurs only after appropriate notice and policy conditions are met.

Hosting status and credit ownership should be handled separately.

An expired publication should not automatically remove the user’s remaining purchased credits.

---

## 13. Embed and Self-Hosted Outputs

Docsflip should distinguish between two different website-related outputs.

### 13.1 Website Embed

The publication remains hosted by Docsflip but is displayed inside another website using generated embed code.

The customer continues to depend on Docsflip hosting.

### 13.2 Self-Hosted or Offline Interactive Package

The customer receives the complete interactive publication package.

This may include:

- HTML;
- JavaScript;
- publication assets;
- viewer files;
- and installation instructions.

The user may host or use the package independently for as long as they control their own infrastructure.

This is not the same as downloading the original PDF.

A PDF download does not fulfil the purpose of an offline flipbook.

The self-hosted or offline output should therefore remain an interactive digital publication and may use more credits because it creates a portable, reusable deliverable.

---

## 14. Credit Ledger and Notifications

Every account should have a transparent credit ledger.

The ledger should show:

- date;
- publication;
- action;
- credits added;
- credits consumed;
- balance after the transaction;
- payment or transaction reference; and
- user or organisation responsible.

Before credits are consumed, the user should approve the cost.

After the action, the user should receive a confirmation such as:

> Your publication has been published.  
> 27 credits were used.  
> Remaining balance: 73 credits.

The system should not silently consume credits.

---

## 15. Inactive Accounts and Purchased Credits

Purchased credits should not expire simply because an account is inactive.

However, Docsflip will need an account inactivity policy.

A possible direction is:

- identify accounts with no login or activity for 12 or 18 months;
- send inactivity notices;
- request the user to log in to keep the account active;
- archive drafts or non-published materials after sufficient notice;
- preserve the recorded credit balance;
- provide an account-recovery process; and
- establish a much longer legally and commercially appropriate period before permanent closure.

The final policy must distinguish between:

- purchased credit ownership;
- promotional credit expiry;
- inactive account administration;
- published-document hosting expiry;
- content retention;
- and legal or compliance obligations.

## 17. Individual and Organisation Models

### 17.1 Individual Publishing

Individuals and smaller organisations may:

- open a free account;
- purchase Docsflip Credits;
- create publications;
- select optional outputs;
- and pay only when they publish or add a publication enhancement.

### 17.2 Organisation Workspace

Larger organisations may prefer a subscription-based workspace because they operate with budgets, teams, procurement processes, and reporting requirements.

An organisation subscription may include:

- a managed workspace;
- multiple users;
- role-based permissions;
- approval workflows;
- pooled credits;
- monthly or annual credit allocations;
- consolidated billing;
- publication libraries;
- organisation branding;
- expenditure reports;
- advanced analytics;
- priority support;
- and invoice or purchase-order payment options.

The subscription should not hide usage.

The organisation should still be able to see how credits are consumed by publication, team member, department, or project.

### 17.3 Enterprise and Institutional Arrangements

Large publishers, institutions, or public bodies may require:

- contract pricing;
- custom credit allocations;
- volume commitments;
- invoicing;
- tailored support;
- custom hosting;
- integrations;
- and negotiated service terms.

## 18. Geographic and Payment Direction

Docsflip should be positioned and designed for Africa rather than defined as an East African product.

East Africa will remain the initial launch pad.

The expansion model should be progressive.

Docsflip may begin with the countries and payment methods that can be supported reliably, then expand through country configuration rather than architectural rebuilding.

Potential payment methods include:

- debit and credit cards;
- mobile money;
- M-PESA and other local mobile-payment services;
- bank payments;
- payment links;
- and invoice-based organisational payments.

Pesapal may be assessed as an initial payment partner because of its African payment integrations, but no provider should be made a permanent architectural assumption before commercial, technical, geographic, settlement, compliance, and support capabilities are validated.

The platform should use a payment abstraction that allows additional providers to be introduced over time.

---

## 19. Initial Product Scope

The first commercially meaningful version of Docsflip should remain focused.

The initial flow should enable a user to:

1. create an account;
2. upload a PDF;
3. preview the flipbook;
4. see page count and publication cost;
5. buy or use credits;
6. select optional outputs;
7. approve credit consumption;
8. publish the document;
9. receive a shareable link;
10. share or embed the publication;
11. view basic analytics;
12. see credit history; and
13. manage hosting status.

The first version should avoid becoming a broad publishing suite before this core transaction works reliably.

---

## 20. What Docsflip Is Not

Docsflip is not intended to be:

- a general document-storage platform;
- a PDF editor;
- a conventional monthly subscription flipbook service;
- a clone of Flipsnack, Heyzine, or another international product;
- a free unlimited hosting service;
- a system that charges for every click or account action;
- a platform designed only for agencies;
- or a product limited to East Africa.

## 25. Concept Statement

Docsflip is an Africa-focused digital publishing platform that allows organisations, publishers, institutions, and creators to transform PDF documents into interactive, shareable digital publications.

It replaces rigid subscription tiers with a transparent credit model in which users publish when they need to and pay only for the outputs they choose.

Docsflip will begin from East Africa, expand across the continent, progressively integrate African payment systems, and use the Founder Engineering Framework to guide disciplined engineering when the product model is ready for implementation.

> **Turn any PDF into a shareable digital publication from as little as $1. Publish when you need to. Pay only for what you use.**
