## 3. Major Architectural Decisions

| Decision                                        | Rationale                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product Identity in narrative, not table        | Table retained for attributes; narrative added for meaning                |
| Philosophy and Principles separated             | Philosophy = enduring beliefs; Principles = actionable policy             |
| Product Scope expanded with explicit boundaries | Prevents scope creep and clarifies document ownership                     |
| Boundary Principles added (§7.3)                | Guides future scope questions between CON-001, COM-001, BIZ-001, USR-001  |
| Product Decision Framework created (§12)        | Ensures future decisions remain consistent with the Product Foundation    |
| Success Definition separated from non-success   | Clarifies what constitutes product success vs business/commercial success |
| Source Material Attribution added (§14)         | Ensures traceability to Legacy Concept without granting it authority      |

## 4. Assumptions

1. The Product Philosophy and Principles established here are expected to be durable across the product's lifetime.
2. The user categories defined in §8 are sufficient for USR-001 to expand into detailed personas.
3. The ecosystem relationships described in §10 are consistent with the Hybrid Dependency Model in MP-001.
4. The scope boundaries (§7) will prevent BIZ-001, COM-001 and downstream documents from being pulled into product foundation territory.
5. The Product Decision Framework does not replace Founder governance — it guides routine decisions and surfaces constitutional ones.
